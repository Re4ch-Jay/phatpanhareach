## 4 Years at Cambodia Academy of Digital Technology

Four years. When I first walked through the doors of the Cambodia Academy of Digital Technology (CADT), four years felt like an eternity stretching out ahead of me. Now, looking back, it feels like it passed in the time it takes to compile a project. This is my reflection on a journey that shaped not just my skills, but the person I've become.

### Year One: The Beginning

I arrived knowing very little. A bit of curiosity, a lot of nervous energy, and a vague sense that technology was where I wanted to be. The first year was about foundations — the syntax of my first programming languages, the logic behind algorithms, and the humbling realization of just how much there was to learn.

Those early days were filled with small victories: the first program that ran without errors, the first bug I tracked down on my own, the first time a concept finally *clicked*. Each one felt enormous. I made my first friends in lecture halls and computer labs, bonded by shared confusion and shared excitement.

### Year Two: Finding My Footing

By the second year, the fog began to clear. I started to understand not just *how* to write code, but *why* certain approaches worked better than others. Object-oriented programming, data structures, databases — the pieces started fitting together into something coherent.

This was also the year I began to find my community. I joined the Coding Club, surrounded myself with people who were just as obsessed with building things as I was, and discovered that the best learning often happened outside the classroom — in late-night debugging sessions and impromptu discussions about the latest frameworks.

### Year Three: Building and Leading

The third year was about taking ownership. Projects grew more ambitious, deadlines more real, and the stakes felt higher. I took on leadership in the Coding Club, learning that guiding others is its own kind of education. Teaching a concept forces you to understand it deeply, and mentoring younger students reminded me of how far I'd come.

It wasn't all smooth. There were projects that broke the night before they were due, group dynamics that tested my patience, and moments of doubt about whether I was good enough. But every challenge left me a little more capable, a little more resilient.

### Year Four: Coming Full Circle

The final year carried a bittersweet weight. The skills that once felt out of reach had become second nature. I could look at a problem and see the shape of a solution. I built projects I was genuinely proud of, collaborated with people I deeply respected, and started thinking seriously about the career ahead.

There's a strange feeling in the last year — the awareness that something meaningful is coming to an end even as a new chapter begins. The campus that once felt overwhelming now felt like home.

### What CADT Gave Me

Beyond the technical knowledge, CADT gave me things I never expected:

- **Friendships** that will outlast any framework or programming language.
- **Confidence** to tackle problems I don't yet know how to solve.
- **A mindset** of continuous learning, because technology never stops moving.
- **A community** of people who believe in the power of digital innovation to change Cambodia and the world.

### Looking Forward

As I close this chapter, I'm filled with gratitude — for the teachers who pushed me, the friends who carried me through hard times, and the version of myself who decided to take this path four years ago.

CADT wasn't just where I studied. It's where I grew up, professionally and personally. The journey doesn't end here; it simply moves to a new platform. But I'll always carry these four years with me.

Thank you, CADT. For everything.
